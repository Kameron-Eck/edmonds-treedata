City of Edmonds, Washington · Briefing for an incoming contractor

Temporal Tree-Canopy Pipeline

Twenty-four years of aerial imagery, one hand-labelled year, and per-tree histories

This pipeline turns 18 aerial imagery acquisitions spanning 2000–2024 into per-year canopy maps and, ultimately, a life history for each individual tree crown in the city. This page sets out what it produces, how data moves through it, how it is measured, and—plainly—which parts are finished and which are not.

Every figure here is drawn from the project's own documentation and measured outputs. Where the internal record is inconsistent, or a measurement has not been made, this page says so rather than estimating.

01

What the project produces

Three deliverables, at three different stages

The end product is not a map. It is a per-tree record of when each crown existed, built from a stack of canopy maps. The two map products below feed that record and are deliverables in their own right.

1

Per-crown temporal validity intervals

◻ Not built

For each of the 222,435 individual tree crowns delineated across Edmonds, a pair of dates stating over what span of the record that crown can be asserted to exist. This is the headline deliverable.

Produced by the temporal-linking and analysis stages (Phases 6–8), which are designed and scoped but not built. The crown geometry it will attach to already exists; the per-year evidence it needs is in production now.

2

Binary canopy masks, one per acquisition

▶ In production ⚠ Provisional

A canopy / not-canopy raster for every one of the 18 acquisitions, each at that imagery's own native resolution—nothing is upscaled for segmentation. Every acquisition in the established series now has a canopy surface over its full imagery footprint, and each of the 17 re-trained years has been scored against a reference the model never trained on. (2020, the anchor, is scored against its own hand labels instead.)

Provisional rather than finished: the accuracy figures are mid-revision pending a reference-definition question (section 05), and the training labels carry an open question set out in the Phase 4 band.

3

Per-crown instance polygons, high-resolution years

✓ 2020 complete ◻ Other 8 years not built

Individually separated crown outlines for the 9 acquisitions fine enough to resolve one tree from its neighbour (2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024). Coarser acquisitions receive a canopy mask only—at those ground resolutions crown boundaries are not reliably separable.

The 2020 layer—the 222,435 crowns—is complete and anchors everything else. The other eight years are a separate per-year stage that has not been built.

All of it is anchored to 2020. 2020 is the only year with human-drawn labels: roughly 3,000 hand-traced crown polygons over five training sites. Every other year in the record is taught from a model's reading of 2020. That single fact drives the design of the pipeline and the whole of its evaluation strategy.

02

Two conventions to know before touching any label

Non-obvious, and load-bearing

Validity-interval semantics

What a pair of dates on a crown means. The interval is a statement about the evidence, not a guess about the tree.

  • Present in 2000 valid_from = 2000
    valid_to   = 2020
  • Absent in 2000 valid_from = 2020
    valid_to   = 2020
    an out-of-interval negative
  • Unsure or unreviewed IGNORE

Ignore is not a third class of tree. An unsure crown is subtracted from the region polygon entirely, so it contributes neither a positive nor a negative to anything downstream.

Three-state mask supervision

Training masks carry three values, not two:

  • 0BackgroundNot canopy.
  • 1CanopyCanopy.
  • 255IgnoreUnknown — excluded from the loss entirely.

Why it exists: a pixel nobody has verified is not evidence of absence. Forcing unsure pixels into "background" would teach the model that unlabelled canopy is not canopy—and that error would compound across 18 years which all inherit their labels from one.

Label corrections are add-only: a correction may add canopy or add ignore, but may never turn canopy into background.

04

The pipeline, top to bottom

Read downward
Inputs

What goes in

● Source data

Aerial imagery

18 acquisitions across 15 calendar years, 2000–2024, from four providers: King County, City of Edmonds, Snohomish County, and NAIP (the USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program). Study area roughly 7.5 × 10.6 km.

Height

A lidar-derived canopy height model (CHM) from a circa-2016 survey, available to the model as an optional fourth input channel alongside red, green and blue. It was added because grass was the dominant source of false alarms.

References and labels

Roughly 3,000 hand-traced crowns on the 2020 imagery—the only human labels in the project. Plus independent land-cover products used for evaluation only; they are never a training source.

Phase 0

Instance segmentation — delineate the crowns

✓ Complete

A U-Net (an encoder–decoder convolutional network) is trained on the hand-traced 2020 crowns to predict a distance-transform surface: for each canopy pixel, how far it sits from the edge of its own crown. A watershed segmentation run on that surface separates crowns that touch.

Output: 222,435 individual crown polygons on the 2020 imagery. This layer is the spatial backbone of every later stage.

Phases 1 – 1D

Spectral features and active learning

✓ Complete

Under each fixed 2020 crown polygon, per-band statistics and vegetation indices are extracted from every one of the 18 acquisitions, giving each crown a spectral time series. An active-learning loop—auto-label, sample, human review, retrain a classifier—was run over those features.

This is the one place upsampled imagery is used, and only so that a fixed polygon can be measured on a coarse image. No segmentation anywhere in the pipeline runs on upsampled pixels.

Phase 2

Data preparation

✓ Complete

Imagery validation, the year-by-site coverage matrix, and overlay quality assurance—establishing which training sites are genuinely visible in which acquisitions before any year is trained.

Phase 3

The 2020 base semantic model

✓ Complete

The same network, retrained with a binary canopy / not-canopy output on the hand-annotated 2020 imagery. This checkpoint is the parent of every per-year model that follows.

Validated by leave-one-site-out cross-validation (LOSO) — each of the five training sites is held out in turn, so no site is ever scored by a model that saw it:

MetricValueWhat it measures
IoU (intersection over union) 0.7299 ± 0.0413 Overlap between predicted and labelled canopy, divided by their union.
AUROC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) 0.9396 ± 0.0190 How well the model ranks canopy above non-canopy, independent of any threshold.

These are scored against the hand labels on held-out sites. They are the strongest numbers in the project, because 2020 is the only year where a human drew the truth.

Phase 4

Per-year semantic fine-tune — the active workstream

▶ Active

The 2020 checkpoint is fine-tuned independently onto each of the other 17 acquisitions, in two stages: first the decoder alone with the encoder frozen, then the whole network at a lower learning rate. Each year is trained and inferred at its own native resolution. The output per year is a canopy probability surface covering that acquisition's full footprint, thresholded into a binary mask.

The pipeline branches by resolution tier. Tile density, the early-stopping metric and—most consequentially—the source of the training labels all differ by tier. That distinction drives most of the pipeline's behaviour:

Fine
e.g. 2013, 2017, 2020, 2022, 2024
Designed label source: per-site hand-traced crown polygons, projected onto that year's imagery.
Medium
e.g. 2005, 2007, 2009
Designed label source: per-site hand-traced crown polygons, projected onto that year's imagery.
Coarse
e.g. 2000, 2016, the NAIP years
Designed label source: the citywide 2020 canopy mask—too few tiles fall inside the small training sites at this ground resolution.
⚠ Current practice differs from the design

Every year is presently trained against the citywide 2020 mask, coarse tier or not. The per-site crown polygons the fine and medium tiers are designed to use are not currently trustworthy: a 14,476-crown human review of them was started and never completed, so those labels remain provisional. Until that review is finished, the tier branch above describes the design, not what is running. This is one of the two reasons the per-year results are marked provisional.

A second caution about the labels: the citywide 2020 mask is itself a model prediction, not hand truth. It carries the model's own blind spots forward into every year that learns from it.

Phases 5 – 8

Instance years, temporal linking, analysis

◻ Not built

Designed and scoped; no code written. Outline only:

Repeat Phase 0's crown delineation on each of the other eight high-resolution acquisitions.

Re-extract per-crown spectra under the canonical crown set rather than the 2020-only set.

8 · Temporal analysis

Change classification, per-year canopy statistics, lifecycle analysis, and the handoff package.

05

How the results are measured

The part that distinguishes this project

One constraint generates all the others: only 2020 has human labels. Every other year is taught from a model's prediction of 2020. So the obvious way to score a year—compare its mask to the 2020 mask reprojected onto it—is circular: real canopy change between that year and 2020 is counted as model error. Those numbers exist in the project's records, and they are never the headline.

Three rules the project holds itself to

  • Leave-one-site-out is the only honest split. The effective independent sample is about five forest sites, not the tile count. Tiles from one site are not independent of one another, so a random tile split inflates every metric it produces.
  • Headline accuracy is scored against an independent reference — a land-cover product the model never saw and was never trained on — not against its own training labels. For the near-infrared-bearing years a second, structurally different reference is also built, from NDVI (normalised difference vegetation index) combined with lidar height, so that "green" alone is not mistaken for "tree".
  • Never score contested ground. The two independent references disagree with each other on 15–17% of pixels, every year. Inside that band there is no truth to be right or wrong about, so the project reports both references and treats them as brackets rather than averaging them into one number.

What the model gets wrong, and where

The consistent finding is that the model is precise but under-predicts: it rarely calls canopy where there is none, and it misses real canopy. Two structural drivers of those misses have been measured, and both survive the confound tests run against them:

Height

Detection rises steadily with canopy height — roughly 0.16 below 5 m to 0.93 above 30 m. The 5–15 m band alone holds 53% of all misses: young, ornamental and understory trees, which is exactly the suburban population a city inventory cares most about.

Crown edges

The outer 2 m of crowns is about 16% of canopy area but carries ~42% of all misses, replicated across two independent years. Most of it is genuine canopy rather than reference bleed, so it calls for boundary-aware supervision, not a threshold change.

Why the per-year figures are provisional, and why they are not printed here. Two reference-definition issues are under review. The two vintages of the independent land-cover reference turn out to belong to different class-scheme generations, so a cross-year comparison spanning them compares two definitions of canopy, not two years of trees. And one class in the newer scheme, which appears to mean "paved surface beneath a tree canopy", is currently charged against the model as a false alarm — whereas under the canopy definition this project has adopted, a tree over pavement is canopy.

Resolving that is a definition change followed by a re-score. It is expected to raise precision; the direction of the effect on recall is genuinely not predictable in advance, and the project has declined to guess. Until it is settled, per-year accuracy figures would be quoted out of date. The honest evaluation exists, it is independent, and the current numbers can be walked through in the meeting.

The open decision that gates the strongest evidence

The most rigorous instrument available — stratified human photo-interpretation with area-adjusted estimates and confidence intervals, the standard method for map accuracy assessment — is built and tooled but has not been run. It is blocked on a written canopy definition, which is a judgment call rather than a measurement: a sample can only reproduce a definition it has been given. One decision has been taken (mid-height woody vegetation counts as canopy, recorded as its own interpreter class so the choice stays reversible); the rest are open. The sample design itself has been simulated and is adequately powered for the definition question.

06

What the imagery actually is

Characterised and documented, not assumed

A 24-year stack from four providers is not a uniform sensor record, and it is worth being explicit about that before anyone assumes otherwise. Each point below was measured from the files rather than read off a configuration value or a metadata field. The variability is known, and it is the reason the evaluation is deliberately conservative.

  • Ground resolution ranges from about 5 cm to 60 cm across providers and years.
  • The grid is not the resolution. Measured by edge response, the imagery is oversampled by roughly 1.3× to 4.0× — the pixel spacing is finer than the detail the image actually resolves. Two consequences that matter in practice: one acquisition delivered on a 20 cm grid resolves detail only at about 80 cm, coarser than another year sitting on a 40 cm grid; and the city's four "5 cm" products were flown at 3 inches (7.6 cm), so the finer grid is a delivery choice rather than extra information. Nominal resolution tiers made this series look cleaner and better ordered than it is.
  • Colour is not comparable across providers. Over identical ground, in the same year and the same season, a naive greenness index reads 0.11 on one provider's imagery and 0.89 on another's. Within a single provider the same index drifts by a factor of about seven across the series — from processing and colour balance alone, not from vegetation. Any greenness-based trend computed across this stack would report a large, steady and entirely artefactual canopy decline. Within-year comparisons are unaffected, because the cast is global to an image.
  • Four of the 18 acquisitions carry a near-infrared band; the rest are visible-only. That limits how many years can carry an NDVI-based independent vegetation reference, which is the main lever available on the reference-disagreement problem described above.
  • The earliest year is not ordinary RGB. The 2000 imagery is a natural-colour derivative of a colour-infrared capture: the sensor recorded green, red and near-infrared, blue was never captured, and an algorithm synthesised a blue channel to approximate a natural-colour look. This is the documented root cause of that year sitting at the extreme end of the colour drift above.
  • Footprints differ. Historically some acquisitions covered as little as ~42% of the study area while others covered all of it, so a "citywide" figure derived from one of them was in fact scoped to a band. An acquisition campaign opened in August 2026 is closing those gaps by re-acquiring from the providers' own source services; the most-cited short year now covers 100% of the city boundary, and a fifth near-infrared acquisition has been added.
  • Acquisition dates were not recorded in the image files at all. They have since been recovered from provider archives, each with a citation and a verbatim quote, and dates without a traceable source were deliberately left as unknown rather than inferred. Several years remain a window rather than a day — including the 2020 anchor, whose flight window is bounded but not pinned to a single date. Sun angle and phenology are therefore only partly controlled across the record.
07

How it runs

Reproducible and auditable
  • Two planes. All code and configuration live in version control; all data — imagery, models, masks, tiles, quality-control outputs — lives in a separate managed store. Compute environments pull code from version control rather than from the data store, so the code that produced any given result is identifiable.
  • Where work runs. Training and full-extent inference run on cloud GPUs. Quality control, label building and raster diagnostics run locally and need no GPU.
  • Write discipline. Large outputs are written to local disk first, verified by size and checksum, then copied into the data store. Every processing step writes a log, and measured outputs are harvested back into version control as text, so a number in a document can be traced to the run that produced it.

Terms used above

AUROCArea under the receiver operating characteristic curve — a threshold-free measure of how well a model ranks positives above negatives.
CHMCanopy height model — a raster of vegetation height above ground, derived from lidar.
GSDGround sample distance — the ground size of one pixel, which as section 06 notes is not the same as the resolution an image actually achieves.
IoUIntersection over union — overlap between predicted and reference canopy divided by their union.
LOSOLeave-one-site-out cross-validation — each training site is held out in turn, so no site is scored by a model that saw it.
NAIPThe USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program — one of the four imagery providers in this record.
NDVINormalised difference vegetation index, computed from red and near-infrared bands. It detects vegetation, not specifically trees, which is why height is combined with it here.
Semantic segmentationClassifying every pixel as canopy or not, without separating one tree from another.
Instance segmentationDelineating each individual crown as its own object.
Circular metricA score computed against labels that were themselves derived from the model's own earlier output — it measures agreement with itself, not accuracy.
