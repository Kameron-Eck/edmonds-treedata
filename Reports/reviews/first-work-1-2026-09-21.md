# Label-error type and semantic segmentation of aerial imagery

## Scope
This review asks how the type of label error — in particular a relative geometric shift between a label mask and the imagery it supervises, as against random pixel noise and segment corruption — affects a semantic segmentation model trained on high-resolution aerial imagery. Every citation in this review names a VERIFIED line of the brief for this workstream; nothing outside that brief is cited. It reads one 2022 conference paper and no other work: no study of historical-archive georeferencing, of orthorectification of pre-digital aerial photography, or of training-data error in Earth-observation machine learning generally was read, and no work in any language other than English was read. It makes no assessment of canopy accuracy for any year of the Edmonds archive, and no claim about how inter-epoch co-registration residuals behave in that archive or about any quantitative error threshold for it.

## Aggregate ranking of label errors
Taken over all classes, the paper ranks the three corruption modes by how much each degrades model performance: "Among all three types of label errors, the errors caused by relative shift affect the model performance the most, followed by
segment errors and random label noise." [Maiti_2022_effect-label-noise-semantic p.6 #01a0c263-3a57-71a0-99db-53d78f24af5f]

## Tree class reverses the ranking
For the tree class, relative shift is the mode the models withstand best: "Regarding tree class, the models are fairly stable
against label errors induced by relative shift" [Maiti_2022_effect-label-noise-semantic p.5 #01a0c263-3a21-7d1f-8acf-b44f2f8368fc]

The class-specific ordering for trees therefore runs opposite to the aggregate one, with segment errors and random noise the worse modes: "Regarding tree class, the models are fairly stable
against label errors induced by relative shift; however, they perform comparatively poorly against segment errors and random
label noise." [Maiti_2022_effect-label-noise-semantic p.5 #01a0c263-3a21-7d1f-8acf-b44f2f8368fc]
