## 1. What is verified

Every number here was either measured by this project's own tools or re-run from recovered
code today. Nothing in this section is inherited on trust.

### 1.1 The model

| | |
|---|---|
| Honest recall, 2016 | **.6636** (not .6844 — the old figure used a clipped reference) |
| Series recall range | .55–.80 across 9 scored years |
| Precision | .78–.92, consistently high — the model **under-predicts**, it does not over-call |
| Model strength vs honest recall | **no correlation.** IoU .49–.76 and AUROC .938–.954 across years, honest recall unmoved. Better models do not close the gap |

### 1.2 Where the misses are — three levers, each measured

1. **Height.** Recall is a monotonic function of canopy height: ~.16 below 5 m rising to
   ~.93 above 30 m. The 5–15 m band holds 53% of all misses. **Survives every confound test
   thrown at it** — reference agreement, crown geometry, and CHM measurement error (the last
   can only *flatten* a real curve, never create one; adding the literature's ~3 m error
   costs 5% of the spread).
2. **Crown perimeter.** The outer 2 m of crowns is ~16% of canopy area but carries **~42% of
   all misses**, replicated across 2016 and 2021s. 93–95% of edge misses carry CHM ≥3 m, so
   they are real canopy, not reference bleed. Only ~⅓ are threshold-recoverable in 2016 and
   fewer in 2021s — the rest needs boundary-aware supervision.
3. **Operating point.** 52–72% of missed forest sits near threshold in the cross-sensor
   years. Genuinely cheap to move, but it costs precision and does not touch the deep misses.

These are **additive, not competing** — the height staircase survives inside crown interiors
(spread +.31) essentially unchanged from the edge (+.31).

### 1.3 Resolution

Recall vs **effective** resolution (measured by edge response, not assumed from the grid):

| effective | 12.9–13.7 cm | 25.5 cm | 26.1 cm | 57.1 cm | 80.7 cm | 110.8 cm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| recall | .7422 / .7401 | .6605 | .6048 | .6136 | .6346 | .5480 |

The **extremes separate** (~19 pp from 13 cm to 111 cm). The **middle does not order** —
2005 at 80.7 cm beats 2009 at 26.1 cm. Nominal GSD tiers made this look cleaner than it is.
Three explanations for the 2000/2002 deficit have now failed: nominal GSD, spectral
sharpness, effective resolution.

> **⚠ 2021k and 2023 are NOT on this table's footing.** Every year above was scored against
> the **full-coverage** 2016 reference (91%). The two 2021-epoch years — 2021k **.6059** and
> 2023 **.6510** — were scored against `ccap_2021_hires_lc.tif`, which is still the **clipped
> 53.1%** copy, because no full-coverage hi-res 2021 *land cover* exists (NOAA's 2021 land
> cover is 30 m). On this project's own measurement the clip costs **2–5 pp of recall**, so
> both are probably understated.
> **Do not read 2023's .6510 against 2013's .7422 as 10 cm variability** — most of that gap
> is the reference, not the model.
> **The fix:** score 2021-epoch years against the full-coverage NOAA **canopy** product
> (`wa_2021_ccap_v2_hires_canopy.tif`, 1.14 m, class 1 = tree), which is a purpose-built
> canopy layer rather than stand-based forest classes. That is a different definition, so it
> is a re-baseline, not a patch — see §1.4.

### 1.4 The reference problem

- The two references disagree on **15–17% of pixels, every year**. Scoring inside that band
  is meaningless — this is the single most common way this project has misled itself.
- Latent-class modelling (no gold standard) puts true canopy prevalence at **~.29** across
  four years, on C-CAP's total, not the NDVI reference's **.377**.
- **The gap is one population, not a scatter.** 33% of NDVI-reference canopy is what NOAA's
  purpose-built product calls neither tree nor shrub: median height **6.0 m**, 88.7% ≥3 m.
  Young/ornamental crowns, hedgerows, understory — the suburban population.
- **Shrubs are not the answer** (1.25% of the grid) and **no height cut separates it**
  (≥3 m keeps 89% of the disputed population).

## 3. Blocked on a human decision

### U1 — the written canopy definition. **This is the only thing blocking Phase 3.**

Both work threads independently ended here. It is a judgment call, not a measurement, and
the evidence is now assembled: `Scripts/canopy_definition_PROPOSAL.md`, six decisions with
recommendations. **D2 is DECIDED (Kam, 2026-08-20): mid-height woody vegetation COUNTS as
canopy, recorded as its own interpreter class (reversible) — this reverses the draft
recommendation; the headline lands on the NDVI-reference side and the 5–15 m misses count
fully against the model.** D1 and D3–D6 remain open (D1 is now a calibration detail under
the adopted D2, no longer an arbitration).

**What changed about it today:** D1 is mis-posed. It asks for a minimum *height*, but the
disputed population is 88.7% above 3 m, so no height cut separates it. The real question is
**crown form / minimum crown size** — is a 6 m ornamental or hedgerow a tree? That is D2.
**Decide D2 first.**

**What it is worth:** ~6 pp of city canopy, i.e. more than twice the entire policy gap.

### Tier 1 — decisions and cheap fixes

1. **Write the canopy definition (U1).** Everything downstream is gated on it. Start at D2.
2. **Decide on the area estimator.** Counting thresholded pixels is threshold-sensitive by
   **17.3 pp** and sits −5.71 pp at the deployed 0.5. The policy debate turns on **2.6 pp**,
   so the estimator's bias is more than twice the policy-relevant difference. A threshold-free
   stratified alternative is documented and unbiased in simulation. Site:
   `phase3_semantic_dev.py:1722`. *(Verified by re-run today.)*
3. **Build raster overviews** (`gdaladdo`). Cheapest performance win available — every
   full-raster QC tool currently reads entire multi-GB files for a decimated view. This is
   why the rescores took 30–60 min each.
4. **Add `*.npz` to `.gitignore`** beside the existing `*.tif`/`*.pt` list.

### Tier 2 — local measurement, no GPU

5. **Is the softness ours?** Every King file is EPSG:3857 and reprojection blurs. 2000 is
   2.8× oversampled, 2005 is 4.0×. If our own mosaicking introduced that, native-projection
   sources recover detail **no retraining can**. Cheap to check, high payoff.
6. **Verify the 88–93% "real miss" chain.** The on-building split that feeds it reproduces
   exactly, but the full chain has not been re-run. If it holds, the honest numbers are *too
   kind*, and the "38.7% unmeasurable" framing is wrong.
7. **Extend `phase4_data_inventory.py`** to check band count and fill values, which would
   have caught the empty 1936 file and the single-band mislabels automatically.

8. **Era-matched 2005 lidar — a third reference, at stand scale.** *(acquired 2026-08-22;
   specs, densities, classes and file locations: `IMAGERY_FACTS.md` §8.)* PSLC 2005 covers the
   2005 imagery year at **0.17–0.25 pts/m²**; USGS 2016 covers it at **4–5 pts/m²** — a
   **16–29× density gap** that governs every use below. Three-way verdict:

   **8a. USABLE — the best use.** An independent, **era-matched, STAND-scale canopy mask for
   2005** (~5 m cells; at 0.25 pts/m² a 2 m cell holds ~1 point). *Presence does not need the
   apex.* Value: a **third reference sharing no failure mode with C-CAP or the NDVI
   reference**, aimed squarely at the 15–17% reference-disagreement problem in §1.4 — and
   contemporaneous rather than 2016-projected.

   **8b. CONDITIONAL — bounding real 2005→2016 change.** Two preconditions, both mandatory
   **before any change number is quoted**:
   - **Decimation protocol.** Thin 2016 to ~0.25 pts/m², rebuild **both** CHMs identically,
     *then* difference. Sparse lidar under-samples apexes and reads low, so a naive
     difference **manufactures growth everywhere**.
   - **Class harmonisation.** 2016 has Water / Bridge Deck / Ignored Ground that 2005 lacks
     (3 classes vs 6), so the DSM/DTM definitions must be made identical first.
   This is the same failure class as the cross-sensor GRVI and the clipped-reference episodes
   in §2 — write the protocol down first, quote the number second.

   **8c. DROP — re-testing the height staircase on 2005.** A 1–2 m low bias shifts crowns down
   a band, and the 5–15 m bands hold **53% of all misses** (§1.2) — exactly where it bites.

   **Overall: 2005 is a STAND-scale instrument, not a crown-scale one.** The hardest miss
   population is scattered suburban/ornamental crowns (8/8 visually-grounded missed stands were
   suburban), which 0.25 pts/m² cannot resolve.

   **Step one of any of this work: measure realised pts/m² on a central Edmonds tile.** The
   stated and cross-checked densities disagree by ~30% for 2005; do not plan cell sizes off
   either until the local number is known.

   *Related:* a **from-points 2016 CHM** is worth building **only as part of 8b**, never
   standalone — U6 says the payoff for existing findings is small, but 8b requires it anyway,
   so the upgrade comes free with work already needed. The CHM currently in use is a degraded
   convenience product (bilinear-upsampled, uint8-quantised, capped at 50.6 m) and reads
   systematically **low** on conifer apexes — `IMAGERY_FACTS.md` §8.3, including why U6 does
   **not** clear it.

### Tier 3 — needs Colab

8. **Finish the recipe-matched series** — 2019, 2017, 2022 are the last years off-recipe.
   `QUEUE3` is already staged in `phase4_train_queue.py`.
9. **Channel ablation** — the only way to settle what the model actually keys on.
10. **Boundary-aware supervision** — soft/distance-weighted edge labels, targeting the ~42%
    of misses that are crown perimeter.
11. **Radiometric normalization** — now specified as per-image **saturation and channel
    balance**, not brightness matching.

### Do not do

- **A whole-record leaf-off correction.** Phenology explains 2015 specifically and is not the
  series axis — 2016 is the most leaf-*on* year and has the *highest* deep-miss share.
- **Cross-year GRVI comparisons of any kind.** The index drifts .80 → .11 across the King
  series from processing alone.
- **Mixed-recipe cross-year tables.** Recipe swings recall 5.6–12.7 pp with the sign varying
  by year.

## 5. Two sample budgets, and they differ 5×

This is the thing neither work thread had alone, and it is the most common source of
confusion in the older notes:

| question | gap to resolve | budget |
|---|---|---|
| Which reference definition is right? | 8.24 pp | **n=250 suffices** — power ~1.0 at ≤5% interpreter error |
| What is the canopy percentage? | 2.6 pp (policy) | **n=250 fails** — ±4.42 pp. Needs ~**1,221 pts/yr** for ±2.0 pp, and change needs more |

Both verified. **Never quote one as though it settled the other.** The definition question is
cheap; the deliverable number is not.

## 6. Standing rules that keep being re-learned

- **Never score contested ground.** Where the references disagree there is no truth to be
  right or wrong about.
- **Re-score, don't reason.** Three separate "surprising reversals" dissolved this session
  once a confound was controlled. Each time, measuring was cheaper than arguing.
- **Check comparability before treating agreement as corroboration.** Two contaminated
  readings agreeing is not evidence.
- **A config value is a claim; a measurement is evidence.** `gsd_cm`, raster footprints and
  reference coverage were all wrong in the config and right in the files.
- **Stage explicit paths, never `git add -A`.** Parallel sessions share this working tree.
