### statistical
VERDICT: The premise that "5 strips is too few for trend claims" is wrong, and it is wrong for an instructive reason: the wide CIs are an artifact of the estimator, not the layout. `qc/phase4_sector_series.py` estimates each year's LEVEL independently, so 95.8% of the variance it reports is the enormous static north-south canopy gradient (strip means 0.493/0.456/0.243/0.174/0.280) — a quantity that is identical every year and therefore cancels completely from any change estimate. The same 5 strips, differenced per-strip, already deliver decision-grade trend numbers: a strip-replicate slope test on the 13-year citywide_rgb backbone gives ±0.037 pp/yr (df=4, t=2.776) versus the ±13 pp level CI. The design is roughly 300x more precise about the question Kam actually wants answered than the current output makes it look. The real problem is one layer down and adding strips cannot touch it: the `p_adj = p_raw * precision/recall` correction is a single citywide constant per year, it ranges 0.692–2.399 (3.47x) across the record, it carries zero uncertainty into the CI, and it flips the sign of the year-over-year change in 7 of 12 consecutive same-arm pairs and flips the 24-year trend outright (+3.02 ± 0.88 pp on p_raw vs −0.93 ± 3.44 pp on p_adj). Meanwhile p_raw's own +3.02 pp is almost certainly a GSD artifact (40.1 cm in 2000/2002 → 20.05 cm 2005–2009 → 10.03 cm 2013+, with recall rising with resolution). So neither series is yet a canopy claim. The binding uncertainty is calibration bias, which is now an order of magnitude larger than the sampling variance — spending A100 hours on a denser layout would buy a tighter interval around a number whose sign is not established.

FINDINGS:
 - ANSWERING THE BRIEF'S KEY QUESTION: qc/phase4_sector_series.py does NOT exploit the pairing. It emits independent per-year CIs only — the totals writer's columns are [year, tag, P_hat, canopy_ha_sampled, se, ci_lo, ci_hi, n_sectors, is_champion], the variance is the successive-difference term computed within a single year's 5 strips, and T975_DF4 = 2.776 is hardcoded. There is no difference, slope, or repeated-measures estimator anywhere in the file. The per-sector p_adj values needed for pairing are, however, already written to sector_canopy_series.csv — the pairing is a pure post-processing addition requiring zero re-inference.
 - VARIANCE DECOMPOSITION (13-year citywide_rgb arm, p_raw, 5 strips x 13 years = 65 cells): SS_total 1.0447, of which SS_between_strip = 1.0011 (95.8%), SS_between_year = 0.0346 (3.3%), residual 0.9%. Per-strip standard deviation across 24 years is only 0.021–0.031 while the between-strip spread is 0.174–0.493. The strips are near-perfectly re-measured units; this is the textbook case for a paired/fixed-effect estimator.
 - PAIRED CHANGE CIs, measured on all 12 consecutive citywide_rgb pairs (p_raw, same weights, same successive-difference machinery applied to per-strip differences): independent-difference CI ranges ±0.162 to ±0.187; paired CI ranges ±0.0116 to ±0.0471. Median shrink 8.9x, best 14.7x (2019->2021). Every year overlapping every other year is purely an artifact of not differencing.
 - TREND TEST WITH THE EXISTING 5 STRIPS: fit an OLS slope per strip over the 13 citywide_rgb years, then treat the 5 strip slopes as the design replicates. p_raw slopes are +0.00035/+0.00064/+0.00132/+0.00154/+0.00152 per year; area-weighted city slope +0.1260 pp/yr with CI95 ±0.0368 pp/yr (df=4) => 24-year change +3.02 ± 0.88 pp, SIGNIFICANT. The same test on p_adj gives −0.0389 ± 0.1434 pp/yr => −0.93 ± 3.44 pp, not significant and opposite in sign. Decision-grade trend detection is already available; the layout is not the constraint.
 - THE CALIBRATION MULTIPLIER IS THE BINDING CONSTRAINT. precision/recall from qc_indep_report.csv live=1 primary=1 rows gives a per-year multiplier of 2.399 (2003s: prec 0.8153 / rec 0.3399), 0.692 (2006s: 0.4415/0.6378), 0.788 (2011s), 1.740 (2018s), 1.173 (2013) — range 0.692–2.399, a 3.47x swing. It enters as p_adj = p_raw * precision/recall with NO variance contribution, and it is applied as one citywide constant to all 5 strips. Consequence measured: dP_raw and dP_adj disagree in SIGN for 7 of 12 consecutive same-arm pairs (2000->02, 2005->07, 2007->09, 2013->15, 2019->21, 2021->22, 2022->23). Narrowing the CI without fixing this manufactures confident nonsense.
 - THE CI IS CONDITIONAL, NOT TOTAL. Strip-replicate variance captures only SPATIALLY VARYING error. Anything common-mode within a year — the calibration multiplier, the per-year re-chosen threshold (0.4659 in 2005 to 0.5133 in 2000 across the backbone), the GSD jump 40.1 -> 20.05 -> 10.03 cm — is perfectly correlated across all 5 strips, cancels out of the variance, and lands entirely in the point estimate as bias. ±0.037 pp/yr is a spatial-sampling CI conditional on model+threshold+calibration. It must never be quoted without that conditional.
 - PAIRING REQUIRES A CONSISTENT ARM, AND is_champion DOES NOT GIVE ONE. The champion designation mixes model arms across years: 2003s/2006s/2011s/2012s/2018s/2020s are sectors_v1 arms while 2005/2007/2009/2019/2021/2022/2023/2024 are citywide_rgb. Differencing 2003s (mult 2.399, sectors_v1) against 2005 (mult 1.444, citywide_rgb) measures a model change, not a canopy change. The only consistent-arm backbone is citywide_rgb: 2000, 2002, 2005, 2007, 2009, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 (13 years).
 - MEASURED N-S SEMIVARIOGRAM (2013 cover_1m, 1-block-row = 152.874 m units): gamma(153 m)=0.00270, gamma(306 m)=0.00704, gamma(459 m)=0.00856, gamma(~2700 m)=0.0107–0.0134, gamma(~5400 m)=0.0276–0.0290. The gradient has a range around 2.5–3 km. Current strips are 4 block-rows (611 m) tall and spaced 2752 m apart (block_row0 = 11, 29, 46, 64, 83), i.e. successive differences are being taken right at the variogram plateau — the worst possible spacing for a systematic sample.
 - PROJECTED (not measured) level-CI gains from thinner strips at IDENTICAL total sampled area: V = sum(W_h^2) * gamma(spacing). Current sum(W_h^2) = 0.2366 (unequal areas; equal-area 5 would be 0.2000), yielding ~±0.107 on p_raw for 2013. L=10 (2-row strips, 1330 m spacing) ~±0.072; L=15 (1.3-row, 887 m) ~±0.052; L=19–20 (1-row, ~665–700 m) ~±0.047, with t dropping 2.776 -> 2.093. Roughly a 2.3x gain, with clear diminishing returns past L=15. I explicitly DISOWN a naive 3.5x figure I first computed by re-slicing the existing strips into 19 sub-strips: those sub-strips come in 5 adjacent clusters, so 15 of the 18 successive differences are at 153 m lag rather than the design's true ~700 m spacing, which understates variance in the classic systematic-sampling way.
 - STRIPS CANNOT CONTROL THE EAST-WEST MICROCLIMATE GRADIENT AT ALL. Every sector spans the full city width by construction (make_sectors.py takes every block-col intersecting the city in that band, west_col/east_col e.g. 47–58 for S1, 30–58 for S2), so the marine-to-upland transect is averaged inside every stratum. Per-strip pairing IS a strip fixed effect and therefore controls all TIME-INVARIANT composition including the E-W gradient — that part of Kam's concern is already handled by differencing. What is not handled is the citywide-constant multiplier applied to strips with different tree mixes: S4 (weight 0.322, p_raw 0.174, lowest canopy, most ornamental/small-tree) sits precisely in the measured worst-recall regime, so one precision/recall pair misallocates the correction across strips.
 - BRIEFING CORRECTION: the sectors_v1.gpkg 'sectors' layer carries only [id, block_row0, water_frac, n_crowns, sites, geometry] — there is NO C-CAP composition and NO distance-to-shore. sectors_v1.json carries dist_shore_m (min/p50/max) plus area_m2_true/land_area_m2_true/water_frac/west_col/east_col, and grep for any 'ccap' key returns NONE. Post-stratification covariates therefore do not exist yet; computing them is a cheap local CPU job but it is a prerequisite, not an existing asset.
 - HOUSEKEEPING / REPRODUCIBILITY: the on-lake city_canopy_totals_design.csv header reads 'area_ha' while the current script writes 'canopy_ha_sampled', so the published CSV was produced by an older script version — the script's own docstring already flags 'area_ha' as mislabeled (it is canopy hectares, not area hectares). Also, the `if len(ps) == len(sid_order)` guard silently suppresses the city total for any year missing a strip (2016 tag '' has only S3/S4/S5), which is correct behavior but should be surfaced in the CSV rather than only on stdout.

### compute
VERDICT: The AOI layout is not a compute lever, and the "6-9x inference cost cut" is being quoted one level too optimistically. Measured on the 2020s arm (`phase4/logs/phase4_semantic_finetune_inference_2020s_2026-08-25T06-18.log`): 636 s total = 276.3 s staging + 81.9 s unaccounted gap (ckpt load/model build) + 276.7 s inference tick + 1.1 s Drive copy. Only the tick's forward passes (202 s of it, fitted) scale with covered pixels. Staging is layout-independent by construction — `core.py:1406` stages the whole ortho before `core.py:1418` even resolves the rects — and the fitted fixed overhead (~47 s) plus the full-grid prefill (~2 ms/Mpx) are layout-independent too. So the sector cut is 7.1x on the inference tick, ~3.7x at the step level, and on a full arm (the 2016_fx arm measured 59.1 min: labels 0.5 + tile 8.1 + train 45.2 + eval 1.7 + inference 3.6) the difference between the current 5 strips and the most scattered layout I modeled (175 site windows) is ±0.5 min — under 1% of the arm. Choose the layout on statistical grounds; the compute argument is spent. The genuine computational fragilities are elsewhere and both are one-liners: the prob raster is written as a striped LZW GeoTIFF with 1-row blocks and no overviews (verified on two published rasters), which makes every windowed write a full-width read-modify-write and every decimated/QC read a whole-grid decompress; and the queue's VERIFY hard-fails MOSTLY_NODATA below 5% valid, which is the only place a layout change actually breaks rather than costs.
